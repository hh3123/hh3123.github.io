Backpressure in asyncio pipelines

2026-09-12

An unbounded queue turns a slow consumer into a memory leak with extra steps.

When a producer is faster than a consumer, something has to give: throughput, memory, or the producer. An asyncio.Queue() with no bound chooses memory, quietly, until the box falls over.

Bound the queue

queue: asyncio.Queue[Item] = asyncio.Queue(maxsize=1000)

async def produce(items):
    for it in items:
        await queue.put(it)   # blocks when full -> natural backpressure

put() awaiting is the whole trick: the producer now yields when the pipeline is saturated instead of buffering without limit.

Cancellation is the other half

Backpressure only works if shutdown is clean. On SIGTERM, stop producing, let the queue drain up to a deadline, then cancel workers. Tasks that catch CancelledError and keep going are how you get a zombie pipeline that never exits.

Measure the depth

The queue depth is your most useful saturation signal. Export it as a gauge; alert when it sits near the bound for longer than a burst. Throughput charts hide saturation until it is too late, depth does not.
